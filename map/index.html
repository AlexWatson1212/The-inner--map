Step 1 · Choose the area What are you trying to understand?

Choose the closest fit. It does not need to be exact, and it does not say anything about diagnosis—it only sets the starting language.

You can stop at any point

If reading this far is enough for today, that is a complete outcome. Nothing is lost by closing the tab.

Step 2 · Capture the moment What actually happened?

Describe what could have been observed by someone in the room. Leave out names and identifying details.

Optional. A short sentence is enough. If nothing comes to mind, you can leave this blank and continue.

How available did your capacity feel at the time?

This is your reading of the moment, not a measurement.

Step 3 · Notice the conditions What was present around the moment?

Select anything that may matter. Presence does not prove cause, and a long list is not more accurate than a short one.

What is your best current reading of the effect?

Step 4 · Hold more than one reading What might explain this?

Write your own explanations. Two readings are more useful than one, because it keeps the first one from hardening into a fact about you.

Neurodivergence will not be the explanation for everything that is hard, and an explanation that fits one moment may not fit the next.

Stuck? Borrow a sentence and edit it

Selecting one of these adds the wording to whichever box is empty. It is a starting point, not a conclusion, and you can delete it.

Step 5 · Choose a small test What is one reversible change?

A useful experiment can be tiny. “Observe once more” is a valid choice, and so is deciding not to run one at all.

Optional. Deciding what you would notice makes a result easier to read later.

30-second version

          
What might reduce the next ten minutes?

        

        
      

      

        
Choose one, or none. You are not committing to a plan for the rest of the day, and not all
          of these are possible in every situation.

      

      

      

        

          
You only need to make one choice. The next step will appear here.

        

        

  
One possible next step

  
Reduce one demand

  
Choose one thing that could be delayed, shortened, or done less completely. If nothing can move right now, that is information too, not a failure.

  
Three things that take under a minute

  
  • Pick the single task with the least consequence if it slips.
  • Decide what ‘done enough’ would look like for one thing.
  • Tell one person one sentence about what is moving.

  
Wording you can borrow, if it helps

  
“I have less capacity than I expected today. I cannot do the full version of this now. I can come back to it later.”

  
Stop here. This does not need to solve the day, and choosing nothing is
    also a legitimate outcome.



  
One possible next step

  
Reduce one input

  
Lower one source of noise, light, touch, information or conversation for a short period.

  
Three things that take under a minute

  
  • Put one thing on silent, or leave one room.
  • Close one screen, tab or app.
  • Change one physical thing: light, temperature, clothing, position.

  
Wording you can borrow, if it helps

  
“I need less input for a while. I am going to pause this conversation and come back when I can.”

  
Stop here. This does not need to solve the day, and choosing nothing is
    also a legitimate outcome.



  
One possible next step

  
Ask for one support

  
Ask for one concrete thing rather than general help. Asking is not always safe or possible—only use this if it is.

  
Three things that take under a minute

  
  • Ask for a written answer instead of a spoken one.
  • Ask someone to do the first step with you.
  • Ask for a deadline to be confirmed rather than guessed.

  
Wording you can borrow, if it helps

  
“I am overloaded and words are harder right now. Could you help with this one specific part: ____?”

  
Stop here. This does not need to solve the day, and choosing nothing is
    also a legitimate outcome.



  
One possible next step

  
Stop choosing for now

  
Pause non-urgent decisions. Pick one familiar, neutral next action and let that be enough.

  
Three things that take under a minute

  
  • Name one decision that can wait until tomorrow.
  • Do one familiar thing you do not have to think about.
  • Sit down, or lie down, for two minutes without a task.

  
Wording you can borrow, if it helps

  
“I cannot make a useful decision right now. I will pause and reconsider when I have more room.”

  
Stop here. This does not need to solve the day, and choosing nothing is
    also a legitimate outcome.

A stopping point

      
Your map does not need to be complete to be useful.

    

    

      
One specific example is more useful than forcing a final explanation. You can keep the
        summary, take it into therapy, or simply notice whether the experiment changes anything.

      
If an explanation stops fitting, that is not a wasted map. Your answers may change with
        context, health and capacity, and a map that changes is doing its job.
